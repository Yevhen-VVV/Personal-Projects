# Domain 2: Asset Security (10% of the exam)

## D2-001 | 2.1 | 1
Q: Who is responsible for determining the classification of a set of business data?
A)* The data owner
= Correct. The owner, usually a senior manager accountable for the data, decides its classification and the protection it needs.
B) The data custodian
= Custodians implement the protection the owner specifies, such as backups and access settings.
C) The security administrator
= Administrators grant access according to the owner's rules; they do not set the classification.
D) The chief information officer
= The CIO runs IT. Classification is a business decision made by the owner of the data.
>> Owners classify and set requirements; custodians implement; users comply.

## D2-002 | 2.1 | 2
Q: In the U.S. government classification scheme, unauthorized disclosure of which level could reasonably be expected to cause serious damage to national security?
A) Confidential
= Confidential is the level where disclosure could cause damage, without the qualifier "serious".
B) Top Secret
= Top Secret is reserved for exceptionally grave damage.
C)* Secret
= Correct. Secret is defined by serious damage to national security.
D) Controlled Unclassified Information
= CUI is unclassified information that still needs safeguarding; it is not defined by damage to national security.
>> Government levels: Top Secret (exceptionally grave damage), Secret (serious damage), Confidential (damage), Unclassified.

## D2-003 | 2.1 | 1
Q: What is the PRIMARY reason to classify information?
A) To satisfy the evidence requirements of external auditors
= Audits check classification, but the reason to do it is protection, not passing the audit.
B)* To match protection to the data's value and sensitivity
= Correct. Classification lets the organization spend on protection where it matters and avoid over-protecting low-value data.
C) To decide which employees may be monitored
= Monitoring decisions are not the purpose of data classification.
D) To determine the order in which data is backed up
= Backup priority comes from business impact analysis, not classification alone.
>> Classification exists so that controls match the value and sensitivity of the data, cost-effectively.

## D2-004 | 2.1 | 2
Q: A company's quarterly earnings report is classified Confidential while it is being prepared. What should happen to its classification after the results are publicly announced?
A) It should stay Confidential for seven years under the retention policy
= Retention determines how long you keep data, not how sensitive it remains.
B) It should be raised, because the report now has regulatory significance
= Regulatory relevance does not make public information more sensitive.
C) It should be destroyed, since the public version replaces it
= The company may still need its records; declassification does not mean destruction.
D)* It should be reclassified as Public, since disclosure no longer causes harm
= Correct. Sensitivity changes over time. Once the information is public, keeping it under Confidential controls wastes resources.
>> Classification should be reviewed as information ages or loses sensitivity; declassification is part of the program.

## D2-005 | 2.1 | 2
Q: A database server stores both public product information and confidential customer records. How should the server itself be classified?
A) As Public, because most of the data on it is public
= Classifying by the majority of data leaves the confidential records under-protected.
B)* As Confidential, matching the most sensitive data it holds
= Correct. A system is protected at the level of the most sensitive information it stores or processes.
C) It does not need a classification; only data is classified
= Assets such as servers are classified too, so that their handling and controls match what they hold.
D) As Internal, a level between the two types of data
= Averaging the levels under-protects the confidential records.
>> Systems and media take the classification of the most sensitive data they hold.

## D2-006 | 2.1 | 3
Q: Analysts combine several datasets that are each classified Internal. The combination reveals the locations and shift patterns of security guards at every site. How should the combined dataset be classified?
A) Internal, because each source is Internal
= This ignores aggregation: the combination is more sensitive than any of its parts.
B) It should not be classified until the data owner of each source agrees
= Owners should be involved, but the new dataset needs protection immediately.
C)* At a higher level that reflects the sensitivity of the aggregate
= Correct. Aggregation can create information more sensitive than its inputs, and classification should reflect what the combination reveals.
D) Public, because none of the sources is classified Confidential
= Treating the aggregate as less sensitive than its inputs is backward.
>> Aggregation: combining lower-sensitivity data can produce information that deserves a higher classification.

## D2-010 | 2.2 | 2
Q: Backup tapes containing customer records are collected weekly by a third-party courier for offsite storage. Which control BEST protects the data if a tape is lost in transit?
A) A signed courier log at every handoff point
= Logs support chain of custody but do nothing to protect data on a lost tape.
B)* Encrypting the tapes before pickup
= Correct. Encryption keeps the data confidential even when the media is lost or stolen.
C) Using a bonded and insured courier service
= Bonding compensates for losses; it does not protect the data.
D) Locked transport cases
= Locks deter casual access but are easily defeated once the case is in someone else's hands.

## D2-011 | 2.2 | 1

= Locking the room still leaves documents exposed to everyone with access to it.
B) A policy reminding staff to collect printouts promptly

## D2-012 | 2.2 | 2
Q: An employee holding a Secret clearance asks for a Secret document about a project they are not assigned to. What should happen?
A) Release it, because the employee's clearance matches the document's level
= Clearance establishes eligibility only; it does not grant access by itself.
B)* Deny it unless the employee has a need to know for the document
= Correct. Access to classified information requires clearance, need to know and, where applicable, formal approval.
C) Release a redacted version without asking further questions
= Even a redacted classified document needs a justified need to know.
D) Escalate to the security officer to upgrade the employee's clearance
= A higher clearance is irrelevant; the missing element is need to know.
>> Clearance and need to know are both required; one without the other is not enough.

## D2-013 | 2.2 | 2

= Obscuring the filename is not protection.
B) Send it in two halves in separate emails
= Splitting the file does not stop either half from being read.
C)* Use the approved encrypted transfer method to the auditor's verified address

## D2-028 | 2.4 | 2
Q: A company is retiring a batch of solid-state drives (SSDs) that held confidential data. Which sanitization method is NOT effective for them?
A) Physical shredding
= Shredding into small particles destroys SSDs effectively.
B) Cryptographic erase on self-encrypting drives
= Destroying the media encryption key renders the data unrecoverable.
C)* Degaussing
= Correct. SSDs store data in flash memory, not magnetically, so degaussing has no effect on the data.
D) The manufacturer's verified sanitize command
= Vendor sanitize commands, where verified, can purge flash media.
>> Degaussing only works on magnetic media; use cryptographic erase, sanitize commands or destruction for SSDs.

## D2-029 | 2.4 | 3
Q: Hard drives that held sensitive data will be sold to an outside buyer. Under NIST SP 800-88, what is the minimum level of sanitization appropriate?
A) Clear
= Clearing protects against simple, non-invasive recovery and suits reuse within the organization, not media leaving its control.
B)* Purge
= Correct. Purging protects against laboratory-grade recovery and is appropriate when media leaves organizational control.
C) Delete
= Deleting only removes file references; the data remains on the disk.
D) Format
= A standard format may leave data recoverable.
>> NIST 800-88: Clear (reuse inside the organization), Purge (media leaving your control), Destroy (media will never be reused).

## D2-030 | 2.4 | 2
Q: How can a self-encrypting drive be sanitized almost instantly?
A) Overwriting the drive with zeros three times
= Overwriting takes hours and may miss over-provisioned areas on flash media.
B) Deleting the partition table
= The data itself remains intact.
C) Reformatting it with a new file system
= A format does not reliably remove data.
D)* Destroying the drive's media encryption key
= Correct. This is cryptographic erase: without the key, the encrypted data on the drive is unreadable.
>> Cryptographic erase (crypto-shredding) sanitizes encrypted media by destroying the key.

## D2-032 | 2.4 | 3

C) As special category data, requiring explicit consent
= Coding names does not make data special category; that depends on its content, such as health data.
D) As encrypted data, exempt from breach notification
= Pseudonymization is not encryption, and no blanket exemption applies.
>> Pseudonymized data is still personal data; only truly anonymized data falls outside GDPR.

## D2-046 | 2.6 | 1
Q: Help desk staff need to confirm a caller's identity but should see only the last four digits of the caller's Social Security number. Which technique should the application use?
A)* Data masking
= Correct. Masking hides part of the value on screen, showing only what the role needs.
B) Salting
= Salting adds random data to passwords before hashing.
C) Steganography
= Steganography hides data inside other data, such as images.
D) Data compression
= Compression reduces size; it does not hide values.
>> Masking limits displayed data to what a role needs, applying least privilege to the screen.
