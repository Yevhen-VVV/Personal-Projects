## D1-001 | 1.1 | 2
Q: A medical-device maker employs a CISSP who finds a flaw that can make an insulin pump deliver the wrong dose. A sales director tells the CISSP to keep the flaw out of a regulator filing until a fix ships next year. Under the ISC2 Code of Ethics, which canon should guide the CISSP's decision FIRST?
A) Provide diligent and competent service to principals
= The employer is a principal, but service to principals is the third canon and gives way to the first two when they conflict.
B) Advance and protect the profession
= This is the fourth canon. It matters, but it is the lowest in precedence and does not address the danger to patients.
C)* Protect society, the common good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the infrastructure
= Correct. The canons are applied in order, and the first one protects society. A safety flaw hidden from a regulator puts the public at risk, so this canon governs.
D) Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally
= Concealing the flaw would also be dishonest, but when canons conflict the first one, protecting society, takes precedence.
>> The ISC2 canons are applied in order: protect society, act honorably, serve principals, advance the profession. Public safety outranks the employer's wishes.

## D1-002 | 1.1 | 1
Q: A candidate posts a list of questions remembered from a recent CISSP exam to an online forum. Which ISC2 Code of Ethics canon is MOST concerned with protecting the value of the credential itself?
A) Protect society, the common good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the infrastructure
= This canon is about society at large and critical infrastructure, not the integrity of a professional credential.
B)* Advance and protect the profession
= Correct. Leaking exam content devalues the certification for everyone who holds it, which is exactly what the fourth canon protects. It also breaches the exam NDA.
C) Provide diligent and competent service to principals
= Principals are employers and clients. Exam leaks do not primarily harm them.
D) Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally
= Leaking is dishonest, but the canon that specifically protects the credential and the profession is the fourth.
>> Canon 4, "advance and protect the profession", covers actions that undermine the credential, such as sharing exam content.

## D1-004 | 1.1 | 2
Q: A security manager is drafting a code of ethics for the organization. Which step will do the MOST to make the code effective?
A) Publishing it on the intranet with a link in the employee handbook
= Publication makes the code available but does nothing to show it matters or that it will be applied.
B)* Having senior leadership adopt it and visibly follow it themselves
= Correct. A code of ethics works when leadership sponsors it and models it. Tone at the top is what turns a document into a culture.
C) Copying the ISC2 Code of Ethics word for word so it carries professional authority
= The ISC2 code is written for certified professionals. An organizational code must reflect the organization's own values and context.
D) Assigning the security team to investigate every reported violation
= Enforcement matters, but without leadership ownership the code is seen as a security-team rule rather than an organizational value.
>> Codes of ethics, like policies, succeed through senior management sponsorship and visible example.

## D1-005 | 1.2 | 1
Q: A partner signs a purchase order with their private key and sends it to you. Which security objective does the digital signature NOT provide on its own?
A) Integrity
= A signature covers a hash of the message, so any change is detected. It does provide integrity.
B)* Confidentiality
= Correct. Anyone with the sender's public key can verify the signature, and the message itself is not encrypted. To keep it secret it must also be encrypted.
C) Nonrepudiation
= Only the holder of the private key could have produced the signature, so the sender cannot credibly deny it.
D) Authenticity of the origin
= Verifying with the sender's public key proves the message came from the private-key holder.
>> Digital signatures give integrity, authentication of origin and nonrepudiation, but not confidentiality.

## D1-006 | 1.2 | 2
Q: An attacker registers a look-alike domain and sends an unaltered copy of a real invoice, changing only the sender address, so the payment goes to the attacker. The invoice text was never modified. Which security property did the victim fail to verify?
A) Integrity
= The invoice content was not modified, so integrity was not the failure.
B) Availability
= Nothing was made unavailable. The systems worked as designed.
C) Confidentiality
= No secret data was disclosed in this scenario.
D)* Authenticity
= Correct. Authenticity is assurance that the data comes from who it claims to come from. The content was genuine, but its source was not.
>> Authenticity is about the genuineness of the source; integrity is about whether the content changed. One can hold while the other fails.

## D1-007 | 1.2 | 1
Q: Which control MOST directly supports availability?
A) Hashing stored files and checking the hashes weekly
= Hash checks detect changes. That supports integrity.
B)* A load-balanced cluster spanning two sites
= Correct. Redundancy and load balancing keep a service reachable when a component fails, which is availability.
C) Encrypting the database at rest
= Encryption at rest protects confidentiality.
D) Recording every administrator command in an audit log
= Audit logs support accountability and detection, not availability.
>> Availability controls include redundancy, fault tolerance, clustering, backups and capacity planning.

## D1-008 | 1.2 | 2
Q: Two banks authenticate transfer messages with a message authentication code (MAC) computed with a shared secret key. A dispute arises over whether one bank actually sent a transfer. Why can't the MAC settle the dispute?
A) MACs do not detect changes to the message
= A MAC does detect modification. That is its purpose.
B) The MAC does not cover the transfer's timestamp
= Which fields a MAC covers is a design detail; even a MAC over every field cannot show which key holder created it.
C)* Either bank holds the same key, so either could have produced the MAC
= Correct. Nonrepudiation requires that only one party could have created the proof. With a shared symmetric key, both parties could compute the same MAC.
D) The MAC algorithm reveals the key to anyone who sees the message
= A properly designed MAC does not reveal its key.
>> Nonrepudiation needs asymmetric digital signatures. Symmetric MACs prove integrity and authenticity between the key holders, not to a third party.

## D1-013 | 1.3 | 2
Q: Before signing with a cloud provider, an organization reviews the provider's SOC 2 Type II report, its incident history and its answers to a security questionnaire. What does this activity BEST demonstrate?
A)* Due diligence
= Correct. Investigating and verifying a provider before committing is due diligence: knowing what you are getting into.
B) Due care
= Due care is acting reasonably to protect assets, such as applying controls. Here the organization is researching, not yet acting.
C) Risk transference
= Transference shifts the financial impact of a risk, for example through insurance or contracts. Reviewing a report does not shift anything.
D) Risk acceptance
= Risk acceptance is a decision to live with a risk. Investigating a provider before signing is due diligence.
>> Due diligence is researching and verifying (knowing); due care is doing what a reasonable person would do (acting).

## D1-014 | 1.3 | 2
Q: A company applies a vendor's critical security patch to its internet-facing web servers within days of release, as a prudent organization in its position would. What does this BEST demonstrate?
A) Due diligence
= Due diligence is the investigation and planning behind decisions. Applying the patch is the action itself.
B) Defense in depth
= Defense in depth means layering controls. A single patch does not illustrate it.
C)* Due care
= Correct. Due care is taking the reasonable actions a prudent person would take to protect assets. Failing to do so is the basis of negligence.
D) Risk avoidance
= Avoidance means stopping the risky activity altogether, such as taking the servers offline for good.
>> Due care is doing the right thing; failing to exercise it can make an organization negligent.

## D1-020 | 1.4 | 2
